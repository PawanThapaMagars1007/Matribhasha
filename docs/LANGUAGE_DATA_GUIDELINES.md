# Language data guidelines

## Vocabulary, grammar, and sentence content

Contributors should cite a source and keep distinct meanings, dialect/variety, and usage contexts as separate records. A translation is a reviewed relationship between source and target vocabulary, not an assumed one-to-one equivalent. Do not publish guesses as verified content. Sentence translations require their own verification status. Attach only approved, consent-recorded audio. Exercises must use stable token or choice IDs and an answer composed from those IDs. New Phase 4 content remains under review until a qualified reviewer verifies and publishes it. Do not add invented seed vocabulary or translations.

- Collect preferred names, spellings, translations, classification, and orthography with community input and attributed sources.
- Treat language, ethnicity, caste, and identity as distinct concepts. Do not merge distinct languages based only on geography.
- A language may have multiple varieties and writing systems, changing conventions, or an oral tradition. Do not assume Devanagari, a standardized variety, or an alphabet.
- Use a language code or family only when verified; otherwise leave it empty. Mark uncertain claims for review and preserve source information.
- Do not automatically publish community submissions. Language and script publication requires an expert-reviewed verification status.
- Preserve original Unicode text without forced transliteration. Test combining marks, grapheme clusters, fonts, search, copy, and paste.
- Do not invent descriptions, alphabets, pronunciation, translations, or audio. Learning materials should be sourced and reviewed by qualified contributors.
- For recordings, document speaker consent, ownership, attribution, allowed use, and deletion process before upload.
- Respect community restrictions on stories, recordings, cultural knowledge, and reuse licenses.
- Development seed entries are drafts with no asserted native names, codes, families, or scripts. Verify them before publication.
- A learning unit may be a grapheme, syllable, sign, or other community-defined orthographic unit. Distinguish it from a phoneme and keep categories specific to its language and script.
- Character names, transliterations, examples, IPA and contextual pronunciation must have an attributable source and reviewer. IPA is labelled as verified only after review.
- Audio needs speaker permission, attribution, source, and review. Label voice type clearly; never describe generated speech as native-speaker audio. Only reviewed recordings are public.
- Do not infer stroke order. Freehand practice may show a reference character without claiming stroke-order guidance, handwriting recognition, or scoring.
- Learner voice recordings and writing strokes remain local unless a separate, explicit, consent-based upload flow is added.
